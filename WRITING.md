# Writing

Ali's direction, November 9, 2026: direct, easy to understand, no fluff, and
closer to how he writes. This applies to the hub and every project page.

## Voice

Write like a person explaining work they know well. Use plain words, active
verbs, and short paragraphs. Contractions are fine. Be confident about what
exists and specific about what does not.

- Start with the work or question. Skip the scene-setting paragraph.
- Say what Ali did, what the result means, and what the visitor can try.
- Use first person for Ali's work. Credit team work and inherited code clearly.
- Explain technical terms when they help. Do not replace precision with slogans.
- Keep limitations next to the claim they qualify. State the limit once.
- Keep employer stories general without adding confidentiality disclaimers.
- Do not add slang, anecdotes, opinions, or achievements to imitate a voice.

## Cut

- No em dashes, including escaped characters and HTML entities. Use a period,
  comma, colon, or parentheses when needed.
- No inflated adjectives such as groundbreaking, revolutionary, or world-class.
- No stock openings about today's rapidly changing AI landscape.
- No filler such as delve into, unlock the potential, or seamlessly integrate.
- No repeated lists of abstract nouns where a concrete action would be clearer.
- No automatic "not just X, but Y" framing or sweeping lessons after every example.

## Examples

Instead of "I build trust systems that show their work":
"I build AI systems and test where they fail."

Instead of "My work connects evaluation design, safety policy, and production
engineering":
"I build evaluations and safety checks for AI features."

Instead of "Explore an artifact-backed checkpoint observatory":
"Move through training and see what the model learned and forgot."

## Review

Read the page aloud. Cut anything that sounds like a pitch instead of an
explanation. Ask whether a recruiter can tell what Ali actually did and whether
the technical detail supports that account.

The automated copy check catches punctuation and a few stock phrases. It does
not certify good writing. Scientific artifacts, frozen protocols, source
quotations, and evidence records must not be rewritten in ways that change
their meaning or history.
