# Research Extension Standard

Updated November 7, 2026. A deployed portfolio page is a distribution milestone,
not evidence that its research extension is complete.

Every Cornell extension needs a project-specific contribution brief:

1. **Useful question:** who could use the answer, and which decision changes?
2. **Current context:** dated primary sources reviewed through November 2026,
   closest related work, and what remains unanswered. Do not claim novelty
   without support or cite work published after the review cutoff.
3. **Substantive extension:** identify what changes beyond the original class
   project. New styling, API wrappers, or newer model names alone do not count.
4. **Testable claim:** suitable baselines, ablations, uncertainty, and a result
   that would contradict the hypothesis. A useful negative result counts.
5. **Evidence:** separate development cases from held-out evaluation; preserve
   frozen protocols, source rights, model/data versions, and reproducible
   artifacts. An unrun study cannot be presented as a contribution.
6. **Interaction:** let a visitor inspect or change something meaningful about
   the question. There is no mandated layout, stack, or tab count.
7. **Limits:** distinguish historical coursework, later measurements, synthetic
   demonstrations, and planned experiments. State access and compute limits.

Existing experiments remain frozen. A revised hypothesis, dataset, model
comparison, or protocol needs a dated extension or deviation before execution;
do not silently modernize an experiment after inspecting its outcomes.

## Professional Case Studies

House IQ and Cornell Tech SETS are professional/practice stories, not public
copies of employer/client systems. Describe contribution, reasoning, and
tradeoffs at a general level. Keep internal metrics, findings, transcripts,
customer data, proprietary code, prompts, vulnerabilities, and remediation
details private. Public sources establish only what they actually report.

Newly authored examples can explain a general principle, but must be marked as
independent illustrations, never client incidents or performance evidence.
